# Privacy and Ethics

## The data is fully synthetic

Every record in every table is generated by a computer program from documented probability models. No real student records, LMS exports, institutional databases, government registries, or external personal datasets are consulted, referenced, or included. Nothing resembling a real person, a real institution, or a real course exists in the output.

## No direct identifiers

EduTrace-Open does not generate:

- Names, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses.
- Government-issued identifiers (national ID, passport, social security numbers, etc.).
- Dates of birth.
- Institutional enrolment numbers that link to real people.
- IP addresses, MAC addresses, or device fingerprints.
- Photographs, audio, video.

Learners are identified only by opaque synthetic IDs (`lrn_<N>`).

## No sensitive demographic attributes

EduTrace-Open does not generate gender, race, ethnicity, caste, religion, disability status, sexual orientation, or any other protected demographic attribute. Digital-access categories (`laptop`, `broadband`, etc.) are synthetic technical conditions; they are **not** proxies for disability, income, race, or any protected attribute, and must not be treated as such.

`synthetic_learning_preference` (`video_first`, `reading_first`, etc.) is a simulated behavioural label. It is not a claim about any real learning style, cognitive ability, or protected characteristic.

## What "synthetic" does not mean

- **Synthetic does not mean anonymous.** If a downstream model is trained on this data and applied to real data, that model is not "differentially private" or "anonymous".
- **Synthetic does not mean realistic.** Nothing here supports claims about real populations, real institutions, or real educational processes.
- **Synthetic does not mean private by construction.** We do not implement differential privacy, k-anonymity, or any formal privacy mechanism. The dataset is synthetic by construction; that is a different guarantee.

## Modelling assumptions are not empirical claims

Every behavioural relationship in the dataset (see [simulation_assumptions.md](simulation_assumptions.md)) is a documented **assumption**, not an empirical finding. In particular:

- Simulated interventions do not demonstrate real intervention effectiveness.
- Simulated digital-access constraints do not indicate real disparities.
- Simulated academic trajectories do not indicate real student outcomes.

## Downstream use

If you publish research using this dataset, you must describe it as a synthetic dataset with documented modelling assumptions. Your conclusions are about the model, not about real higher education. Do not claim generalisation to real populations without external validation on real data.
